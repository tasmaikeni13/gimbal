\section{Results at 125M parameters and 2.5B tokens}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Tuning.}
\Cref{fig:tuning} shows the mean final validation loss of the two tuning seeds against the peak
learning rate. The selected rates are AdamW \LrAdamw{}, SOAP \LrSoap{} and Gimbal \LrGimbal{}, with
$\beta_2=\KnobAdamw$, $\beta_s=\KnobSoap$ and $\alpha=\KnobGimbal$. SOAP tolerated twice Gimbal's
learning rate: at $1.2\cdot10^{-2}$ both had a gradient-norm spike in the first 100 steps, and only
SOAP recovered fully. Gimbal preferred a much faster frame than its default $\alpha=0.05$; its
tuning loss improved at every step of the $\alpha$ grid (\cref{app:tuning}).

\paragraph{The decision.}
\Cref{tab:main} lists every confirmatory run and \cref{tab:paired} compares Gimbal with each
competitor on the same seed. Gimbal's final validation loss is lower than AdamW's on both seeds,
by \DiffAdamwSeedTwo{} and \DiffAdamwSeedThree{} nats, far beyond twice the pooled bootstrap
standard error (\TwoPooledSe{}); it reaches AdamW's final loss after \TokensToAdamw{}B of the
2.5B tokens (token multiplier \MultAdamw{}) and, despite its slower step, in less wall-clock time.
Against SOAP the differences are \DiffSoapSeedTwo{} and \DiffSoapSeedThree{}: of opposite sign,
of the order of the seed-to-seed spread (\PooledSeedSd{}), and far below the threshold. Gimbal's
training step takes \MainStepGimbal{}~ms against SOAP's \MainStepSoap{}~ms. By the predeclared
rule Gimbal beats AdamW on loss (\BeatsLossAdamw) and on wall-clock (\BeatsWallAdamw), but not
SOAP on loss (\BeatsLossSoap) or wall-clock (\BeatsWallSoap); the headline claim that it
outperforms SOAP is \Headline. The test split, read once after the decision, gives the same
picture: mean test loss \TestAdamw{} (AdamW), \TestSoap{} (SOAP) and \TestGimbal{} (Gimbal).

\paragraph{The trajectory.}
The final losses hide how the runs got there (\cref{fig:curves}). On the evaluation subset Gimbal
was below SOAP at \AheadEvals{} of the evaluations (seeds 2 and 3), by up to \MidLead{} nats near
the middle of the runs, and SOAP caught up while the learning rate decayed. AdamW, too, was below
SOAP in the middle of training (at \AheadEvalsAdamw{} evaluations, by up to \MidLeadAdamw{} nats)
before SOAP passed it for good. The mid-training lead therefore says more about SOAP's high peak
learning rate, which costs it in the middle and pays off as the rate decays, than about Gimbal's
frame; Gimbal's lead was more persistent than AdamW's but not larger. The comparison the protocol
fixes is the final loss, and there Gimbal and SOAP are equal within noise.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
The premise that the likelihood frame needs, gradient variances that are not Kronecker-separable
in the rotated frame, fails at this scale as it did on the small model. The noise-corrected
non-separability index of Gimbal's own variance average has median \KappaMainAttn{} in the
attention matrices and \KappaMainMlp{} in the MLP matrices at the end of training (the raw index,
\KappaRawMainAttn{} and \KappaRawMainMlp{}, is almost entirely sampling noise), against the
predeclared threshold of 0.05 (\cref{fig:kappa}); the empirical-Bayes factor is accordingly about
\ShrinkMainMlp{}, so Gimbal's score weights are essentially the separable ones that KL-Shampoo uses.
A cruder estimate from 16 gradients at the final weights, whose noise correction assumes Gaussian
gradients, is larger (median \ProbeKappa{} over the probed matrices; the last MLP projection is the
largest). On held-out validation gradients, each run's own frame fits better than a pooled frame
estimated from 16 fresh gradients at the same weights in \ProbeOwnBetterGimbal{} matrices for
Gimbal and \ProbeOwnBetterSoap{} for SOAP; at step 1{,}000 (one seed, an exploratory probe fixed
before the runs) Gimbal's frame was the better of the two in this sense in \ProbeMidOwnBetterGimbal{}
matrices and SOAP's in \ProbeMidOwnBetterSoap{}. These probes compare each frame with a reference
at its own weights, not the two optimizers' frames with each other, and they do not show that the
better frame caused Gimbal's lead during training.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Including tuning, the repaired trials and diagnostics, the study used \ChipHoursAdamw{} (AdamW),
\ChipHoursSoap{} (SOAP) and \ChipHoursGimbal{} (Gimbal) TPU~v4 chip-hours; Gimbal's share includes
its replaced first tuning trials and the diagnosis of \cref{app:diag}. A repeated tuning run with
the same seed and configuration reproduced every training loss bit for bit.
