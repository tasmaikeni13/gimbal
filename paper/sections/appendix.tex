\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Complete proofs, the proof audit (quantifiers, divisions, degenerate cases) and the history of every
revised statement are in \texttt{theory/gimbal\_theory.md}; the main steps are as follows.

\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:J}.} With $C=(Q_L\otimes Q_R)\diag(\mathrm{vec}D)(Q_L\otimes Q_R)^\top$,
$\mathrm{KL}=\frac12[\tr(C^{-1}S)-mn+\log\det C-\log\det S]$ separates over coordinates, each
minimized at $D_{ij}=(d_U)_{ij}$; non-negativity of the remainder is Hadamard's inequality applied
to the rotated $S$, whose diagonal is $d_U$.

\emph{Lemma~\ref{lem:score} and Theorem~\ref{thm:fisher}.} Under $Q_L\mapsto Q_L(I+\Omega)$,
$Z\mapsto(I-\Omega)Z$ to first order, which gives the score. Its second moment at the true frame
keeps only the terms $\E[Z_{ij}^2Z_{kj}^2]=D_{ij}D_{kj}$, giving $F^L_{ik}$; products of scores of
different pairs, or with a variance score, contain an odd power of an entry and vanish. Each
summand of $F^L_{ik}$ vanishes iff $D_{ij}=D_{kj}$.

\emph{Theorem~\ref{thm:efficiency}.} Item 1 is the delta method for the eigenvector perturbation of
$\hat L_w$ (the off-diagonal entry over the eigen-gap). Item 2 is Cauchy--Schwarz with
$r_j=w_j(D_{ij}-D_{kj})$, $r_j^2=f_jg_j$. Item 3 follows by fixing one row; item 4 by a $3\times2$
array whose two row pairs demand incompatible weights.

\emph{Theorem~\ref{thm:flow}.} The expected score vanishes when cross moments vanish; near the
optimum it equals the Fisher information times the displacement, and the natural-gradient step
divides it out; item 3 is the AR(1) recursion.

\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:shrink} and \ref{prop:center}.} Both risk identities are
$(c-1)^2S+c^2\nu$, minimized at $S/(S+\nu)$; for the innovation, $E[W(W\had A)^\top]$ is diagonal for
noise $W$ with independent entries, so only the mean's rank-one term survives in the skew part.

\emph{Theorems~\ref{thm:retraction}--\ref{thm:descent}} are algebraic identities, checked in Lean.

\section{Diagnosis of an early instability}
\label{app:diag}

In the first tuning trials Gimbal used Adam's momentum $\beta_1=0.9$ while SOAP used its official
$0.95$. At peak learning rates $\ge1.5\cdot10^{-3}$ Gimbal's gradient norm rose to 5--20 and its loss
increased for about 40 steps around the end of the warm-up; the runs recovered but finished about
0.03 nats behind SOAP. Before any configuration was frozen, the tuning sweep was paused and the
following runs were made at learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, seed 0, for the first 300 of the 2{,}500
tuning steps (same schedule), changing one setting at a time; the repair (SOAP's $\beta_1$ for
Gimbal) was fixed before the last three were seen (decision D-008 in the repository).

The instability needs the lower momentum: it disappears with $\beta_1=0.95$, and SOAP is unstable
with $\beta_1=0.9$ too. Freezing or slowing the frame also removes it, but trains much more slowly.
Transporting the second moments to the new frame at every move (\cref{thm:transport}) does not
remove it, so stale second moments are not the cause. A float64 comparison of the distributed
training step with a per-matrix loop rules out an implementation error.

\section{Novelty check}
\label{app:novelty}

Searches of arXiv, OpenReview and conference proceedings (2026-10-06, repeated on the final
method on 2026-10-07; the queries and hits are listed in \texttt{research/literature/frontier.md}) for optimizers that estimate a Kronecker frame
by joint diagonalization, common principal components, EASI-type relative gradients or
Pham--Cardoso non-stationary source separation; for empirical-Bayes shrinkage of second moments
toward Kronecker-separable structure in optimizers; and for centred frame statistics. No overlapping
method was found; closest are KL-Shampoo/KL-SOAP~\citep{lin2025klshampoo} (Kronecker-product
likelihood), TurboSoap (a blog post tracking the factors' eigenvectors by matrix products), core
shrinkage~\citep{hoff2023core} (statistics, not optimization) and Stein-rule gradient shrinkage.
Patents and non-English venues were not searched; ``apparently distinct'' is the strongest
statement this supports.
