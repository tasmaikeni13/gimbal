\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \emph{Scale and seeds.} One model size (125M), one data set, one token budget (about one
Chinchilla), two seeds per optimizer in the confirmatory runs; the decision rule is built for that
design, and differences below the seed-to-seed spread cannot be resolved.
\item \emph{Competitors.} The TPU study compares AdamW, SOAP and Gimbal only (compute constraint).
On the small model KL-SOAP beat Gimbal and SOAP real-time tied it; neither was run at 125M, nor were
Muon and the other peers.
\item \emph{Horizon.} Learning rates and secondary knobs were tuned at a quarter of the confirmatory
horizon for every optimizer alike; optimal values can drift with the horizon.
\item \emph{Tuning grid.} Gimbal's selected rotation rate lies at the edge of its (once extended)
grid: a faster-moving frame was better at every value tried, so Gimbal may be under-tuned in this
dimension. Its momentum is SOAP's ($\beta_1=0.95$); the first tuning trials with Adam's $0.9$ were
replaced after a diagnosis that was not available to the other optimizers (\cref{app:diag}),
which gave Gimbal more attention than its competitors.
\item \emph{Premise.} The likelihood frame is most valuable when the rotated variances are
non-separable. On real gradients of the small model they were almost exactly separable, and the
diagnostics of the 125M runs (\cref{sec:results}) show where the larger model stands.
\item \emph{Cost.} On the TPU v4 Gimbal's float32 frame moves make its step slower than SOAP's; the
comparison of step times is specific to this hardware, its software stack and our implementation
of both optimizers.
\item \emph{Theory.} The efficiency and rate results are local or asymptotic (\lab{L3}) and the
global convergence of the flow is a conjecture.
\end{itemize}
