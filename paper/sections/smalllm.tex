\section{A small language model: the premise, a failure and its repair}
\label{sec:smalllm}

A byte-level Llama-style model ($d=128$, four layers, sequence length 128, batch 32, 800 steps) on a
FineWeb-Edu sample gives a cheap test on real gradients. Every optimizer received the same
four-point learning-rate grid (factor two, extended at an edge) on seed 0 and was then run on two
more seeds at its selected rate. Ten optimizers were compared in PyTorch.

\paragraph{The default first lost to SOAP.} \Cref{tab:smalllm} lists the outcome. The default of the
time (amortized frame moves every four steps, rotation rate $\alpha=0.02$) lost to SOAP on every seed;
moving the frame every step won on every seed; KL-SOAP was best. Selection runs on separate seeds
isolated two causes. The amortized move matches the per-step flow only to first order in
$k\alpha_t$, and with the bias-corrected schedule $\alpha_t$ is large early: the gap to the per-step flow
was largest at step 100 and never closed. And the 100-step frame memory of $\alpha=0.02$, chosen on
stationary synthetic streams, lagged the drifting statistics of a language model: larger $\alpha$ was
better at every amortization. SOAP's memory knob received the same selection budget. The repair
fixed $\alpha=1-\beta_2$ (one estimation window for the frame and Adam's second moment, SOAP's own
convention) and the adaptive amortization of \cref{sec:method}, chosen by a rule written before the
TPU timings were known: faster-moving variants were better on this model but cost more step time
than the rule allowed (\cref{sec:setup}). Every Monte Carlo gate was re-run with the new default
and still passes.

\paragraph{Confirmation on fresh seeds.} With the learning rate re-selected on seed 20 and seeds
21--22 added, the repaired default is lower than SOAP and AdamW on every seed: mean
\GthreeFiveGimbal{} versus \GthreeFiveSoap{} (SOAP) and \GthreeFiveAdamw{} (AdamW). The margin over
SOAP is small (mean difference \GthreeFiveDiffSoap{}, smallest \GthreeFiveDiffSoapMin{}; one-sided
paired $t$-test with Holm correction $p=\GthreeFiveP$). On the same seeds KL-SOAP reaches
\KillKlsoap{}, better than Gimbal on every seed, and SOAP real-time \KillSoapRt{}, indistinguishable
from Gimbal.

\paragraph{The premise does not hold at this scale.} At steps 100, 400 and 800, 96 independent
minibatch gradients of eight matrices were collected at fixed weights, frames were fitted on half
of them and scored on the other half by the held-out likelihood. The likelihood frame beats SOAP's
pooled frame in \EThreeOneWinsSoap{} of \EThreeOneMatrices{} matrices (median gain
\EThreeOneGainSoap{} nats of held-out log-likelihood). But the non-separability index of the
variances, the share of $\mathrm{Var}(\log D)$ not explained by the additive fit, has median
\KappaMedian{} (largest \KappaMax{}), far below the 0.05 that the predeclared premise required: the
rotated variances of this model are almost exactly separable. The likelihood frame's advantage over
SOAP is therefore the efficiency gain of Theorem~\ref{thm:efficiency}.3 on steep separable spectra,
which KL-factor estimators share: against KL frames the likelihood frame wins in only
\EThreeOneWinsKl{} of \EThreeOneMatrices{} matrices (median \EThreeOneGainKl{} nats). This is the
ordering the theory predicts when $\kappa\approx0$, and it matches the losses above. The
hypothesis' own kill criterion ($\kappa\approx0$ and SOAP real-time at least as good as Gimbal) is met
at this scale.
