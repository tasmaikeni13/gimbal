\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

SOAP~\citep{vyas2024soap} preconditions each weight matrix by running Adam in a rotated frame
$(Q_L,Q_R)$, the eigenbasis of Shampoo's Kronecker factors, and it is one of the strongest
optimizers for language-model pre-training at moderate scale~\citep{wen2025fantastic,
semenov2025benchmarking}. Inside SOAP two estimators answer to two different models. Adam's
second moment is the maximum-likelihood estimate of a free array of variances in the frame; the
frame comes from pooled factors $\E[GG^\top]$, $\E[G^\top G]$, statistics of a
Kronecker-\emph{product} covariance, whose variance array is separable. The two coincide only when
the variances are separable and the factors' eigenvalues are distinct.

This paper asks what the frame should be under SOAP's own model, and whether the answer helps. The
answer to the first question is a classical object: the frame that maximizes the likelihood of the
free-diagonal model makes the rotated second moment as diagonal as possible in the log-determinant
sense, a joint-diagonalization problem from statistics and blind source separation. We derive its
score and Fisher information, and an online estimator, \emph{Gimbal}, that follows it with one damped
natural-gradient step on $O(m)\times O(n)$ per block of steps, using only matrix products after a
short warm start.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item The objective behind SOAP's preconditioner family (the profile KL of
\cref{prop:J}) and an identifiability and efficiency theory for frame estimators: the likelihood
identifies every pair of rows whose variance profiles differ, where pooled factors need the profile
\emph{sums} to differ, and every single-factor estimator, SOAP's and KL-Shampoo's included, loses
statistical efficiency except in special cases (\cref{sec:theory}); \LeanCount{} statements are
machine-checked in Lean.
\item Gimbal (\cref{sec:method}): a likelihood frame flow with empirical-Bayes variance shrinkage
toward the separable fit, an innovation-centred statistic, a pooled warm start and adaptive
amortization; matrix products only after step 50.
\item Evidence at three levels, including where it is negative: Monte Carlo on gradient streams
with known truth (\cref{sec:montecarlo}), where Gimbal's frame is much more accurate than SOAP's
and KL-SOAP's; a small language model (\cref{sec:smalllm}), where the premise that real gradient
variances are non-separable fails, Gimbal beats SOAP narrowly and KL-SOAP beats Gimbal; and a
125M-parameter, 2.5B-token comparison with AdamW and SOAP on a TPU v4-32 under equal tuning
budgets and a decision rule fixed in advance (\cref{sec:setup,sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

The 125M comparison is negative for the method's main claim. Gimbal beats AdamW clearly (final
validation loss \ValGimbal{} against \ValAdamw{}, mean of two seeds) but only matches SOAP
(\ValSoap{}): the paired differences have opposite signs on the two seeds, and Gimbal's step is
\MainStepGimbalOverSoap\% slower on the TPU, so the predeclared headline claim is \Headline. The
diagnostics point to the same cause as on the small model: in the rotated frame the gradient
variances of these models are almost exactly separable, which is the case in which our theory
says the likelihood frame and the pooled factors agree. Along the way we found that Adam's
momentum $\beta_1=0.9$ makes both SOAP and Gimbal unstable early at high learning rates on this
model, a confound that equal-budget comparisons of frame-based optimizers should control.
