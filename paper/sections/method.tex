\section{Gimbal: the maximum-likelihood frame, followed online}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The objective behind SOAP's own preconditioner}
For a frame $U=(Q_L,Q_R)\in O(m)\times O(n)$ write $d_U=\E[Z_U(G)^{\circ2}]$ and let $S$ be the
second-moment operator of $\mathrm{vec}\,G$. The Kullback--Leibler projection of $\mathcal N(0,S)$
onto SOAP's family, minimized over $D$, is
\begin{equation}
  J(U)=\min_{D>0}\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathcal N(0,S)\,\|\,\KRD(U,D)\big)
      =\tfrac12\Big(\sum_{ij}\log(d_U)_{ij}-\log\det S\Big)\;\ge0,
  \label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
attained at $D=d_U$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:J}). The best member of SOAP's preconditioner family
uses the frame that makes the rotated second moment ``as diagonal as possible'' in this
log-determinant sense: a joint-diagonalization (common principal components) problem
\citep{flury1984cpc,pham2001joint}, here restricted to Kronecker frames. Adam's average of
$Z_U(G)^{\circ2}$ estimates $d_U$ for whatever frame is used; the frame is the part SOAP takes
from another model.

\subsection{The likelihood score and its Fisher information}
Perturb the left frame as $Q_L\mapsto Q_L(I+\Omega)$ with $\Omega$ skew-symmetric. With
$A=D^{\circ-1}$, the score of the working Gaussian log-likelihood of one gradient is the
skew-symmetric matrix
\begin{equation}
  E_L=S_L-S_L^\top,\qquad S_L=Z\,(Z\had A)^\top ,
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
and on the right $E_R=S_R-S_R^\top$ with $S_R=Z^\top(Z\had A)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:score}). In the
true frame its Fisher information is diagonal in the rotation coordinates,
\begin{equation}
  F^L_{ik}=\sum_j\Big(\frac{D_{ij}}{D_{kj}}+\frac{D_{kj}}{D_{ij}}-2\Big)
  =\big(DA^\top+AD^\top\big)_{ik}-2n ,
  \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
so the rotation of the pair $(i,k)$ is identifiable exactly when the rows $D_{i\cdot}$ and
$D_{k\cdot}$ differ, while pooled factors need their \emph{sums} to differ (Theorem~\ref{thm:fisher}).

\subsection{The flow}
Gimbal follows the maximum-likelihood frame online with one damped natural-gradient step on
$O(m)\times O(n)$:
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_L=-\alpha_t\,\frac{S_L-S_L^\top}{F_L+\delta n},\qquad
  Q_L\leftarrow\mathrm{polish}\big(Q_L(I+\Omega_L+\tfrac12\Omega_L^2)\big),
  \label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
with entrywise division, the diagonal of $\Omega_L$ set to zero, entries clipped to
$[-\theta_{\max},\theta_{\max}]$ and the spectral norm capped at one; the right side is the mirror
image. The retraction $I+\Omega+\tfrac12\Omega^2$ loses orthogonality only at fourth order and a
Newton--Schulz polish restores it (Theorem~\ref{thm:retraction}); no QR or eigendecomposition is
needed. Dividing by $F$ is Amari's natural gradient~\citep{amari1998natural}, so near the optimum
every pair contracts at the same rate, independent of its eigen-gap (Theorem~\ref{thm:flow}). The
rate follows the bias-corrected schedule $\alpha_t=\alpha/(1-(1-\alpha)^t)$, which makes the frame
a bias-corrected exponential average of per-gradient estimates (Theorem~\ref{thm:schedule}), the
identity that justifies Adam's bias correction.

\paragraph{The variances that weight the score.}
$D$ in \eqref{eq:score} is an exponential average of $Z^{\circ2}$ with the frame's own memory
$\beta_D=1-\alpha$, the profile likelihood with one forgetting factor (Lemma~\ref{lem:profile}),
shrunk toward its separable (additive-in-log) fit by a positive-part James--Stein factor whose
noise level is measured from an interleaved odd/even split of the average
(Proposition~\ref{prop:shrink}, after the core shrinkage of \citet{hoff2023core}). With
separable statistics the weights become KL-Shampoo's efficient ones; with strongly non-separable
statistics they become the free maximum-likelihood variances.

\paragraph{The innovation.}
A gradient with a non-zero mean tilts the uncentred statistic away from the true frame
(Proposition~\ref{prop:center}); Gimbal fits the frame to the innovation $G_t-c_t\hat M_{t-1}$,
where $\hat M_{t-1}$ is the previous bias-corrected momentum and $c_t$ its own James--Stein factor,
$c_t\approx0$ for zero-mean gradients and $c_t\to1$ when a deterministic mean dominates.

\paragraph{Warm start and amortization.}
The frame starts from the eigenvectors of the first gradient's factors and, after $T_w=50$ steps,
restarts from the eigenvectors of the pooled factors of those steps, a one-step estimator in Le
Cam's sense (Remark~\ref{rem:warm}): these are the only two eigendecompositions. The score is
accumulated over blocks of $k_t$ steps and the frame moves once per block, so the retraction,
polish and Fisher matrix are paid once per block. The block length is adaptive,
$k_t=\mathrm{clamp}(\mathrm{round}(K\alpha/\alpha_t),1,K)$ with $K=4$: the amortized step matches
the per-step flow only to first order in $k\alpha_t$, so the early steps, where $\alpha_t$ is
large, are not amortized.

\paragraph{Defaults.}
$\alpha=1-\beta_2=0.05$ (the frame, its variances and Adam's second moment share one estimation
window of effective size 39, SOAP's convention for its factors), $\delta=0.003$,
$\theta_{\max}=0.25$, $K=4$, $T_w=50$, $\beta=(0.9,0.95)$. \Cref{alg:gimbal} summarizes one step.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Per step Gimbal rotates the gradient, rotates the step back and forms the score: three products
of cost $m^2n+mn^2$, the same count as SOAP's rotations and factor updates. Once per block it adds
the Fisher matrix, the retraction and the polish, $O(m^3+n^3)$ products in float32. SOAP adds a
QR decomposition every $f=10$ steps. Gimbal's state is two frames, the momentum, Adam's second
moment, the flow's variance average and its odd half, plus the accumulated scores between moves.
